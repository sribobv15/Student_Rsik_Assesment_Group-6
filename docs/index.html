Project overview

Our project investigates whether previous academic failures, weekly study time, and school absences can help identify students at risk of academic failure. We use a simple, rule-based scoring system to classify students into three categories: Low Risk, Medium Risk, and High Risk. To evaluate the algorithm's performance, we compare its predictions with students' actual final grades (G3), considering a grade below 10 as academic failure. This helps us assess how effectively the algorithm identifies students who may need additional academic support.

Research question: How effectively can previous academic failures, weekly study time, and school absences be used to identify students at risk of poor academic performance?

PROJECT INSPIRATION

Research Motivation and Inspiration

Our project was motivated by the growing importance of learning analytics and the potential of educational data to support students' academic success.

In particular, we were inspired by the book chapter “Be Careful What You Wish For! Learning Analytics and the Emergence of Data-Driven Practices in Higher Education” by Teresa Cerratto Pargman and Cormac McGrath (2021).

The chapter discusses how learning analytics can provide insights into student learning while also highlighting ethical concerns about data-driven practices in higher education.

Building on these ideas, our group decided to explore whether a simple, transparent, rule-based algorithm could identify students who may be at risk of academic failure. We focused on three interpretable academic indicators: previous failures, weekly study time, and school absences.

Our aim is to explore the potential of educational data while recognizing that algorithmic predictions have limitations and should not be used as the sole basis for decisions about individual students.

Academic reference: Cerratto Pargman, T., & McGrath, C. (2021). Be Careful What You Wish For! Learning Analytics and the Emergence of Data-Driven Practices in Higher Education. In Digital Human Sciences. Stockholm University Press. Read the book chapter ↗

Why this topic?

Early identification can help educators consider timely academic support. We chose an interpretable, rule-based approach so that each risk label can be explained without a complex model.

This is an educational prototype, not a tool for making decisions about individual students.

Key takeaway

In our preliminary run, binary prediction accuracy was 84.90%, but the algorithm detected only 12 of 100 actual failures (12% recall). This illustrates why accuracy alone is not enough to judge a risk-identification system.
